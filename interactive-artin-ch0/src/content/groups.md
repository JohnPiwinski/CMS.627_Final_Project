# Introductory Group Theory

## square | Secret moves
### Slide
Turn or flip the marked square. How many distinct arrangements can you find?
### Narration
The colors help you see what moved. The unmarked square has the same shape after each symmetry. Doing nothing is a move too.

## laws | What makes a group?
### Slide
Compose moves and try changing the brackets. What changes if you swap the moves?
### Narration
A group is closed under composition, has an identity and inverses, and its operation is associative. In formulas the rightmost move goes first.

## examples | Clocks and polygons
### Slide
Change the number of corners. Compare a clock step, a turn, a flip, and a token swap.
### Narration
The turns of an n-gon form the cyclic group. Including flips gives the dihedral group of size two n. A permutation moves each token to a distinct place.

## clubs | Subgroup clubs
### Slide
Choose moves that can work together in a smaller group.
### Narration
A subgroup uses the same composition as the big group. Every move in the club needs its undo, and composing two club moves must keep you in the club.

## cosets | Equal piles
### Slide
Choose a club and move it as a whole. Can two representatives make the same pile?
### Narration
Left multiplication gives a left coset. The piles partition the group, and each pile has the same size as the subgroup.

## normal | Normal or not?
### Slide
Try a different representative. Compare left and right multiplication with the same subgroup.
### Narration
A subgroup is normal when every conjugate is the same set. The members may still exchange places.

## quotient | Quotient machine
### Slide
Collapse the piles into tokens. Change the representatives and compare their product piles.
### Narration
When the subgroup is normal, the resulting coset depends only on the input cosets. The original moves remain distinct in the original group.

## translator | Symmetry translator
### Slide
Send two moves through the orientation machine, then compose. Does the order of these two steps matter?
### Narration
A homomorphism preserves composition. The moves mapped to the identity form its kernel.

## action | Actions on corners
### Slide
Pick a corner to follow. Compare the permutation of all corners with where your corner lands.
### Narration
The same move acts on every corner. The identity fixes every corner, and acting by a composition agrees with applying its moves in order.
