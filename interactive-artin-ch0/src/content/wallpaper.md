# Wallpaper Groups

## motions | Four moves
### Slide
Move a marked motif. Try a glide by reflecting and then sliding parallel to the mirror.
### Narration
All four motions preserve distances, but only certain ones preserve a particular pattern. A zero glide is an ordinary reflection.

## fixed | What stays put?
### Slide
Pick a test point in the view, predict where it will land, and test a move.
### Narration
A nontrivial turn fixes its center. A reflection fixes its mirror. A nonzero slide or genuine glide fixes no points. Checking two points illustrates distance preservation.

## art | Pattern in art
### Slide
Compare a decorative source with an exact construction. Look for matching directions of turns and mirrors.
### Narration
A photograph captures a finite part of a design. Wear, perspective, and colors can obscure or change the visible symmetry.

## lattice | Grow a lattice
### Slide
Start with a point, then repeat it along one vector or two. Adjust the basis to see the cell change.
### Narration
All the locations are integer combinations of your vectors. Rank zero, one, and two describe discrete translation groups in the plane.

## infinite | Infinite pattern machine
### Slide
Change the repeating construction, then test a motion against the whole pattern.
### Narration
The crop is only a window onto the ideal infinite pattern. The arrow inside each colored motif shows which way that copy faces.

## restriction | Which turns fit?
### Slide
Superimpose the turned lattice. Can you find a periodic lattice that matches after a 72-degree turn?
### Narration
A planar lattice permits rotation orders one, two, three, four, and six across all possible lattices. A single fivefold motif can still exist on its own.

## gallery | Gallery of 17
### Slide
Open a pattern, pan and zoom, then test moves or compare it with a neighbor.
### Narration
The point group records linear parts. A glide has a reflection-type linear part even if the original pattern has no mirror.

## detective | Pattern detective
### Slide
Test rotations, mirrors, and glides first. Then use your evidence to identify the repeating design.
### Narration
Try a few examples. You can move on whenever you like. Judge exact constructions by their transformations and photographs by visible evidence.

## domains | Cut a repeating tile
### Slide
Cut a triangular piece from a period cell and slide it to the opposite edge.
### Narration
The two tiles cover the plane by the same translations. Interiors are unique, with shared boundaries. With extra symmetries a fundamental domain can be smaller than a period cell.

## crystals | Into the third dimension
### Slide
Add layers to a periodic toy structure and look at the three repeat directions.
### Narration
Lattice sites tell you where a motif repeats. The full symmetry of the result depends on the motif. There are 17 wallpaper types and 230 crystallographic space-group types.
